# Reliable sources and Perennial sources (Wikipedia community, policy and guideline pages, 2024)

- URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Reliable_sources and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Reliable_sources/Perennial_sources
- PDF: not downloaded (wiki pages, no PDF)
- Status: methodology page
- Why it matters for source trust: the largest working system that decides source reliability by open discussion, with a five-level status list and a documented process for demoting a source.

## What it says
Wikipedia's reliable-sources guideline asks for sources with a reputation for fact-checking and accuracy. Reliability is judged on four things: the piece, its creator, the publication and the publisher, and any of the four can affect it. Reliability depends on context, so the same source may be fine for one claim and not for another. More people checking facts and scrutinising the writing makes a publication more reliable. Well-established news outlets are generally reliable for statements of fact. Opinion pieces and headlines are not. Questionable sources have a poor reputation for checking facts or lack editorial oversight. Self-published sources are mostly not acceptable and never for claims about living people. The Perennial sources list records community consensus on sources that come up often, with five statuses: generally reliable, no consensus, generally unreliable, deprecated and blacklisted. Deprecation needs a request for comment and is reserved for sources with a substantial history of fabrication.

## Method
1. For any citation, ask whether the piece, the creator, the publication and the publisher each have a reputation for accuracy; context and the specific claim decide (WP:RS, Overview and Context matters).
2. Treat well-established news organisations as generally reliable for facts; treat editorials, opinion columns and headlines as not reliable for facts (WP:RS, News organizations).
3. Mark a source questionable if it has a poor reputation for checking facts or no meaningful editorial oversight; exclude self-published sources except for narrow cases (WP:RS, Questionable and self-published sources).
4. For recurring sources, hold a discussion at the reliable sources noticeboard. A Perennial sources entry needs a request for comment or at least two significant discussions, each with no fewer than three participants (two if the source is named in the heading) (Perennial sources, header).
5. Record the consensus as one of: generally reliable, no consensus, generally unreliable, deprecated (RfC consensus), blacklisted (spam blacklist for abusive addition).
6. Deprecate only through an RfC, usually at the noticeboard, for sources with a substantial history of fabrication or other serious accuracy issues; deprecated sources should not be used unless there is specific consensus (WP:RS, Deprecated sources).

## Claims we can test against
1. Reliability is a property of the claim-source pair, not of the source alone.
2. A source's rank can be derived from the amount and quality of editorial checking it applies.
3. Opinion content and headlines from a reliable outlet should carry lower trust than its news body text.
4. Third-party raters (Ad Fontes, AllSides) are themselves rated, and Wikipedia does not treat them as reliable, so rater outputs need their own trust weight.
