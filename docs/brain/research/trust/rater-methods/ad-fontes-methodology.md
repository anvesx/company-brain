# Media Bias Chart methodology (Ad Fontes Media, methodology page, 2024)

- URL: https://adfontesmedia.com/methodology/
- PDF: not downloaded (web page, no PDF)
- Status: methodology page
- Why it matters for source trust: a commercial, article-level rating of reliability and bias with a defined scale and multi-analyst panels, so its numbers can be used as labels or compared with other raters.

## What it says
Ad Fontes rates individual articles and shows, then rolls those up to a source score. Each article is scored for reliability on a 0 to 64 scale and for political bias on a -42 to +42 scale, where negative is left and positive is right. Every article is rated by a panel of at least three analysts, one who leans left, one right and one centre. Analysts are trained and their scores are averaged. The source score is a weighted average of its article scores. Reliability above 36 is described as generally good, below 24 as generally problematic, and 24 to 36 as a range of possibilities. The page states counts of over 94,600 articles and shows rated, about 283,800 ratings, and over 4,770 sources. The page is inconsistent on analyst numbers (over 30 in one place, over 40 in another) and on training hours (35 in one place, at least 30 in another).

## Method
1. Sample articles and shows from a source; classify each as news, analysis or opinion.
2. Assign each item to a panel of at least three analysts: one left-leaning, one right-leaning, one centre-leaning. Outliers get larger panels; a second panel reviews if discrepancies remain.
3. Each analyst scores reliability (sub-factors: Expression, Veracity, Headline/Graphic) on 0 to 64 and bias (sub-factors: Political Position, Language, Comparison) on -42 to +42 (scale ranges confirmed on the Natural News rating page, adfontesmedia.com/?p=43834).
4. Average the analysts' scores for the item.
5. Compute the source score as a weighted average of its item scores with a proprietary algorithm on a truncated scale.
6. Reliability bands: above 36 generally good; 24 to 36 mixed; below 24 generally problematic. Certified Reliable badge requires reliability 36 or higher and bias within plus or minus 12 (adfontesmedia.com/?p=80855).
7. Analysts receive 35 initial training hours and 40 ongoing hours per year (figures as stated on the page; the page also states different numbers).

## Claims we can test against
1. A three-person politically balanced panel is enough to give stable article scores (compare with Pennycook and Rand's crowd of hundreds).
2. Reliability and bias are separable axes; a source can be biased but reliable.
3. Source scores aggregated from article scores are stable across samples (the weighting is undisclosed, so this cannot be reproduced).
4. The 36 and 24 thresholds separate outlets that other raters also mark reliable or problematic.
