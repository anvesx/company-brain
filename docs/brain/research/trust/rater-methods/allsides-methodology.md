# Media Bias Rating Methods (AllSides, methodology page, 2024)

- PDF: not downloaded (web page, no PDF)

- Why it matters for source trust: a bias-only rating built from blinded surveys of ordinary Americans plus multipartisan editorial panels, with a numeric -6 to +6 meter and a published confidence level.

## What it says
AllSides rates the political bias of outlets as Left, Lean Left, Center, Lean Right or Right. It does not rate accuracy or reliability. Ratings come from several methods. In a Blind Bias Survey, Americans who have self-rated their own bias read headlines and articles with branding removed and rate the slant; each bias group's average is computed, then averaged across groups. In an Editorial Review, a multipartisan panel of six to nine reviewers from left, centre and right examines the homepage, headlines, articles and photos up to six months back and each gives a number from -6.0 to +6.0. Smaller three-person reviews, independent single-reviewer reviews, third-party data and community votes also feed in, but community votes never decide a rating. Each rating carries a confidence level of Low, Medium or High based on how many methods were applied and how consistent they were. Ratings are licensed CC BY-NC 4.0.

## Method
1. Blind Bias Survey: strip branding from a sample of the source's headlines and articles; participants across the spectrum rate slant; compute the mean per bias group (Left, Lean Left, Center, Lean Right, Right by the Rate Your Bias tool); average across groups. Limitation stated: small snapshot, no photos.
2. Editorial Review: six to nine reviewers from left, centre and right review content up to six months back (Wayback Machine) for slant, spin, sensationalism and story choice; each assigns -6.0 to +6.0; scores are combined into a weighted result. Small Group Editorial Review uses three people, one each left, centre, right. An Editorial Review can override a survey if the panel judges the survey sample unrepresentative.
3. Independent Review: one AllSides reviewer forms a view from common bias types, the outlet's self-description and third-party reports; the lowest level of analysis, used for initial ratings.
4. Third-Party Data: academic or survey research with a transparent method may be considered and is noted on the source page.
5. Community Feedback: users vote agree or disagree; votes are not blind and not normalised; they can trigger a deeper review but never set the rating.
6. Map the meter to a label: Left -6.00 to -3.00; Lean Left -2.99 to -1.00; Center -0.99 to +0.99; Lean Right +1.00 to +2.99; Right +3.00 to +6.00.
7. Assign confidence Low or Initial, Medium, High from the number of methods applied and the consistency of their results.

## Claims we can test against
1. Blinded lay raters and a small balanced expert panel give the same label in most cases (AllSides says surveys and reviews usually agree; no rate is published).
2. Community votes disagree with the official rating often, yet their average rating matches it.
3. Bias and accuracy are separate; an AllSides label carries no information about factuality.
4. Confidence should rise with the number of independent methods, which can be checked against later rating changes.
